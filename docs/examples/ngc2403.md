# NGC 2403: HI with 3DFIT

The HI of the nearby spiral galaxy NGC 2403, observed with the VLA, is a regular rotating disk, many beams across.
This example fits it with {doc}`3DFIT <../tasks/3dfit/index>` and the default method, CLASSIC: the rotation
velocity, the velocity dispersion, the inclination and the position angle of each ring, with their errors. It is
the classic use of BBarolo. A step-by-step analysis of the same cube, with the checks of the fit, is in
{doc}`ngc2403-3dfit`.

## The data

| | |
|---|---|
| Object | NGC 2403, a nearby late-type spiral galaxy |
| Data | HI 21-cm line, VLA (Fraternali et al. 2002), smoothed to a beam of 60 arcsec |
| Cube | 150 × 150 pixels of 16 arcsec, 62 channels of 5.15 km/s |
| Beam | 59.3 × 58.6 arcsec |
| Noise | about 5.5 × 10⁻⁴ Jy/beam; the peak is 0.26 Jy/beam |
| Files | [ngc2403.fits](https://github.com/editeodoro/bbarolo2/raw/main/examples/ngc2403.fits) (5.4 MB), [ngc2403_3DFIT.par](https://github.com/editeodoro/bbarolo2/raw/main/examples/ngc2403_3DFIT.par) |

:::{note}
The cube is contaminated by the HI of the Milky Way disk in the foreground: faint, narrow and patchy emission at
heliocentric velocities from about −10 to +10 km/s, all over the field, which overlaps with the extreme
velocities of the approaching side of NGC 2403 (down to about 15 km/s). It is not part of the galaxy, but it is
detected by the source finder, so part of it is in the mask of the fit (`MASK SEARCH`), where the model of the disk
has no gas.
:::

## Running the example

::::{tab-set}
:sync-group: lang

:::{tab-item} Parameter file
:sync: par

```{literalinclude} ../../examples/ngc2403_3DFIT.par
:language: text
```

```bash
BBarolo -p ngc2403_3DFIT.par
```
:::

:::{tab-item} Python
:sync: py

```python
from bbarolo import FitMod3D

f = FitMod3D("ngc2403.fits", nradii=42, radsep=30, vsys=132.8, xpos=77, ypos=77, vrot=100, vrad=0,
             vdisp=8, inc=60, pa=123.7, z0=10, free="VROT VDISP PA INC", flagerrors=True)
res = f.run(threads=16)     # in memory
res.rings                   # the rings of the second stage, with the errors
res.write()                 # the files of the command line, in output/ngc2403/
```
:::
::::

On our server, with 16 cores (`THREADS 16`, the default), the run takes **52 seconds**, of which about half are
the errors.

## The setup

- **Rings.** 42 rings of 30 arcsec ({ref}`NRADII <par-3dfit-nradii>`, {ref}`RADSEP <par-3dfit-radsep>`), out to
  1260 arcsec, where the emission ends. They are half a beam wide, so neighbouring rings are not independent:
  the rotation curve is sampled twice per beam.
- **Initial values.** The centre (XPOS, YPOS, in pixels) and the systemic velocity are those of the galaxy, and
  are kept fixed. VROT, VDISP, INC and PA are first guesses: they are free
  ({ref}`FREE <par-3dfit-free>` `VROT VDISP PA INC`). INC and PA can move by ±15° from the initial values
  ({ref}`DELTAINC <par-3dfit-deltainc>` and {ref}`DELTAPA <par-3dfit-deltapa>`, the defaults). Z0 is the scale
  height of the disk, 10 arcsec, thin compared with the beam.
- **Two stages.** With INC and PA free, 3DFIT fits each ring with its own geometry, then regularises INC and PA as
  functions of radius and fits VROT and VDISP again ({ref}`TWOSTAGE <par-3dfit-twostage>`, the default). Here the
  regularisation chosen automatically is the median, INC = 55.9° and PA = 123.6°.
- **Errors.** {ref}`FLAGERRORS <par-3dfit-flagerrors>` estimates the errors of the free parameters from the shape of
  the residual function of each ring (see {doc}`../tasks/3dfit/algorithm`).
- **Defaults.** The mask is that of the source finder ({ref}`MASK <par-3dfit-mask>` `SEARCH`), the model is
  normalised to the azimuthal profile of the data ({ref}`NORM <par-3dfit-norm>` `AZIM`), and the beam and the
  channel width come from the header.

## Results

The rotation curve rises to 130 km/s at 450 arcsec and keeps rising slowly, to 140 km/s at the edge of the disk;
the errors of VROT are 3 to 9 km/s. The dispersion falls from about 20 km/s in the centre to 10–12 km/s. In the
first stage the inclination of the rings goes from 55° to 65°, higher in the inner disk, where the rings hold few
independent resolution elements, and the position angle from 113° to 134°.

The model follows the data in all the slices, including the minor axis and the slices off the centre. The blue
contours that the model does not have, at the top of the position–velocity diagrams (−130 km/s from the systemic
velocity, i.e. about 0 km/s) and in the last channel map, are the foreground HI of the Milky Way, as are the small
patches at the edge of the velocity field. The
residuals of the velocity field are within ±20 km/s, with the non-circular motions of the gas, and the residuals
of the dispersion map are large in the centre (beam smearing of the steep inner rotation curve) and in a region
in the south of the disk.
