# NGC 891: the lagging HI halo of an edge-on spiral

NGC 891 is a spiral galaxy seen edge-on, with a thick layer of HI that extends several kpc above and below the
disk and rotates more slowly than the disk: a lagging halo. The example fits it with
{doc}`3DFIT <../tasks/3dfit/index>` and `METHOD GLOBAL`, with a model of two layers: a thin disk and a thick
layer (the halo) whose rotation decreases with the height above the plane. The scale heights of the two layers,
the fraction of gas in the halo, its velocity dispersion and its vertical gradient of rotation velocity are fitted
together with the rotation curve.

## The data

| | |
|---|---|
| Object | NGC 891, an edge-on spiral galaxy, systemic velocity 528 km/s |
| Data | HI 21-cm line, Westerbork Synthesis Radio Telescope, HALOGAS survey (Heald et al. 2011); the deep observations of Oosterloo et al. (2007) |
| Cube | 113 × 153 pixels of 8 arcsec (binned 2 × 2 by BBarolo), 87 channels of 8.24 km/s |
| Beam | 35.1 × 32.2 arcsec |
| Files | [ngc891.fits](https://github.com/editeodoro/bbarolo2/raw/main/examples/ngc891.fits) (6.0 MB), [ngc891_3DFIT_GLOBAL.par](https://github.com/editeodoro/bbarolo2/raw/main/examples/ngc891_3DFIT_GLOBAL.par) |

:::{tab-item} Python
:sync: py

```python
from bbarolo import FitMod3D

f = FitMod3D("ngc891.fits", method="global", specres=0.825, nradii=16, radsep=40,
             xpos="2:22:32.99", ypos="+42:20:52.1", inc=88, pa=203, vsys=528, vrot=180, vdisp=13, z0=3,
             radmaxa=440, z0thick=30, fthick=0.3, vdispthick=32, dvdz=0.8, mask="SEARCH", ltype=3,
             free="vrot vdisp_single z0_single z0thick_single fthick_single vdispthick_single dvdz_single "
                  "fracerr radmaxa",
             priors="vrot=(uniform,30,200) dvdz=(uniform,loc=0,scale=2) radmaxa=(uniform,loc=400,scale=100)",
             flagerrors=True)
res = f.run(threads=16)     # in memory
res.rings                   # the best fit, with the errors (and the columns of the thick layer)
res.fitter.print_stats()    # the free parameters, with the errors
res.fitter.corner_plot()    # the corner plot (a matplotlib Figure)
res.write()                 # the files of the command line, in output/ngc891_1/
```
:::
::::

On our server, with 16 cores (16 processes for the gradient of the minimiser), the run takes **18 minutes**: 16.7
minutes for the fit and the errors, 4132 likelihoods of a model of two layers on 177 000 voxels (the mask dilated by
one beam). This is the slowest kind of model: the thick layer is sampled over several scale heights.

## The setup

- **Spectral resolution.** The channels are Hanning-smoothed, so the resolution is twice the channel width, 16.5
  km/s. {ref}`SPECRES <par-3dfit-specres>` is 0.825 channels, 6.8 km/s: case 9 of
  {doc}`../user-guide/spectral-resolution`, Hanning smoothing of channels whose resolution is one channel.
- **Rings and geometry.** 16 rings of 40 arcsec, a bit more than the beam, out to 640 arcsec. The centre is given in
  sexagesimal coordinates (XPOS, YPOS), the inclination (88°), the position angle (203°) and the systemic velocity
  are fixed: for a disk this close to edge-on the inclination is poorly constrained, and it trades off with the
  thickness of the layers.
- **Two layers.** {ref}`Z0THICK <par-3dfit-z0thick>` adds a thick layer to the thin disk of scale height Z0:
  {ref}`FTHICK <par-3dfit-fthick>` is the fraction of the gas of each ring in the thick layer,
  {ref}`VDISPTHICK <par-3dfit-vdispthick>` its velocity dispersion. Both layers have the vertical profile of
  {ref}`LTYPE <par-3dfit-ltype>` 3, an exponential. The values in the file are the starting point of the fit.
- **The lag.** {ref}`DVDZ <par-3dfit-dvdz>` is the decrease of the rotation velocity with the height above the plane,
  in km/s per arcsec, and acts on the thick layer only; the rotation never reverses (it stops at zero).
- **Free parameters.** VROT in each ring; one value for the whole disk of VDISP, Z0, Z0THICK, FTHICK, VDISPTHICK and
  DVDZ (`_single`); FRACERR, a fractional error of the model; and RADMAXA, the outer edge of the approaching half of
  the disk (see below): 24 parameters ({ref}`FREE <par-3dfit-free>`).
- **One-sided edge.** The HI disk of NGC 891 is lopsided: it extends further on the receding side.
  {ref}`RADMAXA <par-3dfit-radmaxa>` cuts the approaching half at its own radius (440 arcsec to start), while the
  receding half extends to the last ring.
- **Priors.** {ref}`PRIORS <par-3dfit-priors>` sets VROT between 30 and 230 km/s (`(uniform,30,200)`: the
  arguments of `scipy.stats.uniform` are `loc` and `scale`), DVDZ between 0 and 2 km/s/arcsec and RADMAXA between
  400 and 500 arcsec. The others are the defaults: Z0 between 0 and half a beam, Z0THICK from there to 3 beams more,
  FTHICK between 0 and 1, VDISP and VDISPTHICK between 0 and 50 km/s.
- **Errors.** {ref}`FLAGERRORS <par-3dfit-flagerrors>` computes the errors of the Laplace approximation.

## Results

| Parameter | Best fit | At 9.5 Mpc |
|---|---|---|
| VDISP, thin disk | 14.4 ± 1.3 km/s | |
| Z0, thin disk | 10.2 ± 0.6 arcsec | 0.47 kpc |
| Z0THICK, thick layer | 47.8 ± 2.3 arcsec | 2.20 kpc |
| FTHICK | 0.276 ± 0.020 | |
| VDISPTHICK | 34.5 ± 0.9 km/s | |
| DVDZ | 0.766 ± 0.021 km/s/arcsec | 16.6 ± 0.5 km/s/kpc |
| RADMAXA | 434 ± 5 arcsec | 20.0 kpc |
| FRACERR | 0.54 ± 0.02 | |

The rotation curve is between 203 and 217 km/s from 60 to 380 arcsec and declines to 176 km/s in the last rings. The
log warns that the inner rings are near the upper limit of the prior of VROT: the first ring is at the limit itself,
230 km/s, so the prior may be too narrow there. The reduced χ² is 139 without the model error and 1.27 with it: the
data have a high signal-to-noise ratio, and the HI of NGC 891 has structure that a smooth axisymmetric model does not
have (the lopsided disk, the filaments and clouds of the halo), so FRACERR is large (54% of the model). The log also
warns that the Hessian is not positive definite in some direction: the errors of the Laplace approximation are
indicative.

**Comparison with the literature.** At the distance adopted by Oosterloo et al. (2007), 9.5 Mpc (1 arcsec = 46 pc),
the halo of the fit is close to the one of their models, built by hand on the same galaxy:

- **The mass of the halo.** 27.6% ± 2.0% of the HI is in the thick layer; they find almost 30%.
- **Its thickness.** The exponential scale height of the thick layer is 2.2 ± 0.1 kpc; they measure an
  exponential profile with a scale height of 50 arcsec (2.2 kpc) in three quadrants of the halo.
- **The lag.** A vertical gradient of the rotation velocity of −16.6 ± 0.5 km/s/kpc. Fraternali et al. (2005)
  find −15 km/s/kpc; the constant gradients of the models of
  Oosterloo et al. (2007) are −12 km/s/kpc with the dispersion of the disk and −17.4 km/s/kpc with a halo
  dispersion of 25 km/s (the fit gives 34.5 km/s). At one and two scale heights the halo of the fit rotates 37 and
  73 km/s more slowly than the disk, in the range of 25 to 100 km/s of Swaters et al. (1997).
- **The radial variation.** Oosterloo et al. (2007) also find that the lag is stronger in the centre (−43 km/s/kpc
  at R = 0, decreasing to −14 km/s/kpc): here DVDZ is one value for the whole disk (`dvdz_single`).

The thin disk, 0.47 kpc, is thicker than the 0.2 kpc of their inner disk: with a beam of 35 arcsec (1.6 kpc) it is not
resolved, and at the inclination of 88° the projection of the outer disk adds to it (cos 88° × 600 arcsec = 21 arcsec),
so Z0 depends on the fixed inclination.
