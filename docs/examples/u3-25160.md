# U3_25160: Hα at z = 0.9 with METHOD BAYESIAN

A star-forming galaxy at redshift 0.9 observed in Hα with KMOS at the VLT, from the KROSS survey. The disk is
only a few seeing disks across and the signal-to-noise ratio is low: the typical case of high-redshift
kinematics, where beam smearing is strong and the uncertainties are large. The example fits it with
{doc}`3DFIT <../tasks/3dfit/index>` and `METHOD BAYESIAN`: the posterior of the rotation curve, the dispersion and
the geometry, sampled with nested sampling, which gives credible intervals that include the correlations of the
parameters.

## The data

| | |
|---|---|
| Object | U3_25160 (KROSS), a star-forming galaxy at z = 0.896 |
| Data | Hα, KMOS at the VLT, KROSS survey (Stott et al. 2016), fitted with BBarolo by Di Teodoro et al. (2016) |
| Cube | 24 × 24 spaxels of 0.2 arcsec, 16 channels of 1.75 Å (42 km/s) around Hα at 1.245 μm |
| Resolution | seeing 0.65 arcsec (`BEAM`), line spread function of 0.9 channels (`SPECRES`, dispersion) |
| Files | [U3_25160_Ha.fits](https://github.com/editeodoro/bbarolo2/raw/main/examples/U3_25160_Ha.fits) (0.2 MB), [U3_25160_3DFIT_BAY.par](https://github.com/editeodoro/bbarolo2/raw/main/examples/U3_25160_3DFIT_BAY.par) |

:::{tab-item} Python
:sync: py

```python
from bbarolo import FitMod3D

f = FitMod3D("U3_25160_Ha.fits", method="bayesian", beam=0.65, specres=0.90, redshift=0.89615,
             restwave=0.656461, nradii=4, radsep=0.3, vsys=4, xpos=11.6, ypos=12.3, vrot=130, vdisp=20,
             inc=50, pa=305, z0=0.1,
             free="VROT VDISP INC_SINGLE PA_SINGLE XPOS_SINGLE YPOS_SINGLE VSYS_SINGLE FRACERR",
             priors="vdisp=(uniform,loc=0,scale=100) vrot=(uniform,loc=0,scale=250)",
             mask="SEARCH", growthcut=1.8)
res = f.run(threads=16)     # the sampling, in memory
res.rings                   # the median rings, with the 68% intervals
res.samples                 # the posterior samples (equal weights)
res.fitter.print_stats()    # the free parameters, medians and intervals
res.fitter.corner_plot()    # the corner plot (a matplotlib Figure)
res.write()                 # the files of the command line, in output/U3_25160/

# the classic fit instead (the lines commented out in the parameter file)
res_c = f.run(method="classic", free="VROT VDISP", flagerrors=True, adrift=True)
```
:::
::::

On our server, with 16 cores (16 processes for the sampler), the run takes **1 minute 42 seconds**, of which 82
seconds are the sampling (87 000 likelihoods). The sampler is random, so the numbers below change slightly from
run to run.

## The setup

- **Lines and velocities.** The spectral axis is in μm: {ref}`RESTWAVE <par-galmod-restwave>` is the vacuum
  wavelength of Hα in μm, and {ref}`REDSHIFT <par-galmod-redshift>` the redshift of the galaxy, so that VSYS is the
  velocity relative to it (4 km/s to start).
- **Resolution.** The header has no beam: {ref}`BEAM <par-all-beam>` is the FWHM of the seeing, 0.65 arcsec.
  {ref}`SPECRES <par-3dfit-specres>` 0.9 channels is the dispersion of the line spread function, 38 km/s: as large
  as the dispersion of the gas.
- **Rings.** 4 rings of 0.3 arcsec, half the seeing, out to 1.2 arcsec. The rings are not independent: the
  seeing smooths the rotation curve over two of them.
- **Free parameters.** VROT and VDISP in each ring; one inclination, position angle, centre and systemic velocity
  (`_SINGLE`); and FRACERR, a fractional error of the model, because a smooth disk does not reproduce the clumpy Hα
  of a star-forming galaxy: 14 parameters. Z0 (0.1 arcsec) is fixed.
- **Priors.** {ref}`PRIORS <par-3dfit-priors>` sets uniform priors on VROT, between 0 and 250 km/s, and on VDISP,
  between 0 and 100 km/s: wide enough to let the data decide. With channels of 42 km/s and an instrumental
  dispersion of 38 km/s, the data constrain the dispersion of the gas only weakly, and the posterior shows how
  much (see below). The other priors are the
  defaults (INC between 40° and 60°, PA between 290° and 320°, the centre within ±6.5 pixels of the initial one,
  VSYS ±5 channels).
- **Mask.** The source finder ({ref}`MASK <par-3dfit-mask>` `SEARCH`) grows the detections down to 1.8 times
  the noise ({ref}`GROWTHCUT <par-search-growthcut>`). The likelihood uses the voxels of the mask dilated by one
  beam.
- **The classic alternative.** The last lines of the parameter file, commented out, fit the same rings with METHOD
  CLASSIC and VROT and VDISP free. {ref}`FLAGERRORS <par-3dfit-flagerrors>` and
  {ref}`ADRIFT <par-3dfit-adrift>` (the asymmetric-drift correction) are used by that method: with METHOD BAYESIAN
  they are ignored, with a warning. The errors of BAYESIAN are always computed.

## Results

| Parameter | Median and 68% interval |
|---|---|
| VROT, rings 1 to 4 | 106 (−68, +74), 113 (−41, +44), 162 (−40, +38), 142 (−79, +67) km/s |
| VDISP, rings 1 to 4 | 41 (−28, +35), 18 (−13, +19), 14 (−10, +17), 34 (−24, +36) km/s |
| INC | 48.0° (−4.7, +5.1) |
| PA | 299.9° (−3.9, +4.1) |
| VSYS | −2.7 (−4.6, +4.4) km/s |
| FRACERR | 0.44 (−0.07, +0.08) |

The rotation velocity of 110–160 km/s is measured to ±40 km/s in the middle rings; the first ring, inside the
seeing, and the last, at the edge of the emission, are hardly constrained. The dispersion is barely measured: in
the two middle rings the posterior is concentrated below about 50 km/s (medians of 14 and 18 km/s, 95% upper limits
of 50 and 56 km/s), and in the first and last ring it spreads over most of the prior. A dispersion smaller than
the instrumental one and than the channels cannot be measured precisely from these data: a fit that gives it with
a small error would be showing its prior or its starting value. The large FRACERR (44% of the model) says that the
clumpy emission differs from the model of a smooth disk well above the noise: the reduced χ² is 3.66 without the
model error and 1.02 with it.

The classic fit of the commented lines (VROT and VDISP free, the geometry fixed at the initial values) gives
VROT = 100, 125, 134 and 151 km/s, with errors of 6 to 16 km/s. Its errors are much smaller because they are
those of one parameter with all the others fixed, the geometry included: the intervals of BAYESIAN include the
uncertainty of the inclination, of the centre and of the neighbouring rings.

The corner plot shows the correlations that a ring-by-ring fit cannot give: the rotation velocities of
neighbouring rings are anti-correlated (correlation coefficients −0.5 to −0.6: the seeing mixes them), VROT is
anti-correlated with INC (−0.4 for the third ring), and the centre with VSYS (0.4 for XPOS: a shift of the centre
along the major axis mimics a change of the systemic velocity).
