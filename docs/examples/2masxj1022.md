# 2MASX J1022: a long-slit spectrum with SLITFIT

A long-slit spectrum of a massive spiral galaxy at z = 0.09, taken with the Southern African Large Telescope
(SALT) along the major axis, with Hα and the [NII] doublet over a bright stellar continuum. The example fits it with
{doc}`SLITFIT <../tasks/slitfit/index>` and `METHOD GLOBAL`: a 3D disk model seen through the slit, with the
seeing and the instrumental resolution, fitted to the 2D spectrum (position along the slit × wavelength) by
maximum likelihood. The rotation curve and the velocity dispersion come out corrected for the seeing, which a fit of
the line centroids position by position cannot do.

## The data

| | |
|---|---|
| Object | 2MASX J1022, a massive spiral galaxy at z = 0.091 (Di Teodoro et al. 2021) |
| Data | long-slit spectrum, Robert Stobie Spectrograph at SALT; slit 1 arcsec wide along the major axis |
| Spectrum | 230 positions of 0.253 arcsec × 3172 channels of 0.41 Å, from 6100 Å (vacuum wavelengths, in metres) |
| Resolution | seeing 1.5 arcsec FWHM (`BEAM`), instrumental broadening of 1.7 channels (`SPECRES`) |
| Files | [2masxj1022_SALT.fits](https://github.com/editeodoro/bbarolo2/raw/main/examples/2masxj1022_SALT.fits) (5.8 MB), [2masxj1022_SLITFIT.par](https://github.com/editeodoro/bbarolo2/raw/main/examples/2masxj1022_SLITFIT.par) |


:::{tab-item} Python
:sync: py

```python
from bbarolo import SlitFit

f = SlitFit("2masxj1022_SALT.fits", method="global", slitwidth=1, beam=1.5, specres=1.7, redshift=0.09108,
            restwave=[6564.61, 6549.86, 6585.27], relint=[1, 0.17, 0.45], contsub=True, skyresid=True,
            radii="range(2.5,19,2)", inc=71, xpos=110, norm="AZIM", free="VROT VDISP VSYS",
            priors="vrot=(uniform,loc=100,scale=350) vdisp=(uniform,loc=10,scale=150) "
                   "vsys=(uniform,loc=-10,scale=20)",
            likeopts="smooth=(vrot=50,vdisp=1)", outfolder="output/2masxj1022_slit")
res = f.run(threads=16)     # in memory
res.rings                   # the rings of the best fit
res.model                   # the model of the slit (an HDU)
res.fitter.plot_pv()        # the slit, the best model and the residuals (a matplotlib Figure)
res.write()                 # the files of the command line, in output/2masxj1022_slit/
```
:::
::::

On our server, with 16 cores, the run takes **27 seconds**: 23 seconds for the fit of 19 parameters (1368
likelihoods).

## The setup

- **Lines.** The wavelength axis is in vacuum, so {ref}`RESTWAVE <par-slitfit-restwave>` has the vacuum wavelengths
  of Hα, [NII]6548 and [NII]6583, in Å (an axis in metres is read in Å). The model has the three lines at the same
  velocities, with the intensities of {ref}`RELINT <par-slitfit-relint>` relative to Hα, 0.17 and 0.45. The
  velocities are relative to Hα at {ref}`REDSHIFT <par-slitfit-redshift>` 0.09108.
- **Slit and resolution.** {ref}`SLITWIDTH <par-slitfit-slitwidth>` is the width of the slit (1 arcsec) and
  {ref}`BEAM <par-all-beam>` the FWHM of the seeing (1.5 arcsec): the model is the light of the disk smoothed by
  the seeing that falls into the slit. {ref}`SPECRES <par-slitfit-specres>` is the instrumental broadening, 1.7
  channels (0.70 Å, 29 km/s; see {doc}`../user-guide/spectral-resolution`). A slit header carries neither: both are required.
- **Preparing the spectrum.** {ref}`CONTSUB <par-slitfit-contsub>` fits and subtracts a straight line at each
  position, away from the lines, to remove the continuum of the stars; {ref}`SKYRESID <par-slitfit-skyresid>`
  subtracts from each channel the median of the positions without line emission, to remove the residuals of the
  sky subtraction that fall on the lines.
- **Rings.** {ref}`RADII <par-slitfit-radii>` `range(2.5,19,2)` gives 9 rings, from 2.5 to 18.5 arcsec every
  2 arcsec (a bit more than the seeing). The rings start at 2.5 arcsec: at the nucleus, the stellar Hα absorption
  cancels much of the Hα emission once the continuum is subtracted.
- **Geometry.** A slit does not constrain the geometry: the inclination is given ({ref}`INC <par-slitfit-inc>` 71°),
  the angle between the slit and the major axis is estimated from the velocity gradient (180°: the slit is along the
  major axis, approaching side at increasing positions), and the centre along the slit is fixed at pixel 110
  ({ref}`XPOS <par-slitfit-xpos>`, not in FREE).
- **Free parameters.** VROT and VDISP in each ring, and VSYS ({ref}`FREE <par-slitfit-free>`): 19 parameters.
- **Priors.** {ref}`PRIORS <par-slitfit-priors>`: VROT between 100 and 450 km/s, as for a massive spiral; VDISP
  between 10 and 160 km/s; VSYS within ±10 km/s of the redshift.
- **Normalisation.** {ref}`NORM <par-slitfit-norm>` `AZIM` (the default): the flux of the model is an axisymmetric
  profile, fitted to the flux of the lines along the slit. `NORM LOCAL` would leave the flux at each position free
  and fit only the shape of the lines: better for a lopsided flux, see below.
- **Smoothness.** {ref}`LIKEOPTS <par-slitfit-likeopts>` `smooth=(vrot=50,vdisp=1)` regularises the profiles:
  VROT may bend by 50 km/s over one seeing FWHM, a weak constraint, and VDISP by only 1 km/s, a strong one, as the
  dispersion of a disk varies little from ring to ring and is degenerate with the instrumental resolution.
- **Errors.** Without {ref}`FLAGERRORS <par-slitfit-flagerrors>`, METHOD GLOBAL gives the best fit without errors.
  `FLAGERRORS true` adds those of the Laplace approximation, and `METHOD BAYESIAN` samples the posterior.

## Results

The rotation curve is nearly flat, between 266 and 316 km/s from 4.5 arcsec outwards, slowly rising; the first ring,
at 2.5 arcsec, has 160 km/s. The dispersion is 21 to 48 km/s, except in the first ring (119 km/s), where the
unresolved rise of the rotation curve within the seeing broadens the lines, and in the last one, at the lower bound
of its prior (10 km/s; the log warns about it). VSYS is 4.2 km/s. The reduced χ² is 2.7.

```{figure} /figures/examples/2masxj1022_pv.png
The slit (`_slitfit_pv.pdf`): the continuum-subtracted spectrum with the Gaussian fits of the lines at each
```

The model follows the rotation curve of the three lines. The largest residuals are in the flux, not in the
velocities: the lines are fainter than the axisymmetric model at −10 arcsec and brighter at +10 arcsec (the blue and
red patches), because the flux along the slit is lopsided, and `NORM AZIM` gives the two sides the same profile.
The same fit with `NORM LOCAL`, which leaves the amplitude free at each position, has a reduced χ² of 1.24 instead
of 2.62, and the same rotation curve within 2 km/s: here the asymmetry of the flux does not bias the rotation curve.
